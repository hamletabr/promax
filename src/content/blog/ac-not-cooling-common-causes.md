It's 95° in San Jose, your AC is running non-stop, and the house isn't getting any cooler. Before you panic (or sweat through another night), here are the seven causes we find most often on service calls — starting with the ones you can check yourself.

## 1. A clogged air filter — check this first (free)

A packed filter chokes airflow, and low airflow can even freeze the indoor coil. Pull your filter and hold it up to a light: if you can't see light through it, replace it, set the fan to ON to thaw any ice, and give the system a few hours.

## 2. Thermostat settings or dead batteries (free–$)

It sounds silly until it's the fix: make sure it's on COOL, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the batteries are fresh. A blank or unresponsive thermostat can also mean a tripped float switch — which brings us to…

## 3. Clogged condensate drain (~$150–$300)

Your AC pulls gallons of moisture out of the air, and that water leaves through a small drain line. When it clogs, a safety switch shuts the system down to prevent water damage. Quick fix for a technician.

## 4. Weak capacitor (~$150–$400)

The most common electrical failure in hot weather. The capacitor gives your outdoor unit's motors their starting kick; when it weakens, the fan or compressor won't spin up — the system hums but doesn't cool. Cheap part, fast fix.

## 5. Dirty condenser coil (~$150–$350 as part of a tune-up)

The outdoor unit needs to dump heat. Coated in dust, cottonwood fluff, or dryer lint, it can't — and cooling capacity plummets while your bill climbs. This is exactly what an [annual tune-up](/services/air-conditioner-maintenance) prevents.

## 6. Refrigerant leak (~$400–$1,500+)

Low refrigerant from a slow leak means warm air, ice on the lines, or hissing sounds. The refrigerant doesn't "run out" on its own — there's always a leak to find and fix. Beware anyone who just "tops it off" every summer without finding the leak.

## 7. Failing compressor (repair vs. replace decision)

The big one. If the compressor is dying in a 12+ year-old system, replacement is usually smarter than a $2,500+ repair — especially with [rebates on heat pumps](/financing) that cool *and* heat. We'll give you both numbers honestly.
