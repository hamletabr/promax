If your furnace is on its last legs, you're facing the biggest home-comfort decision of the decade: **replace it with another gas furnace, or switch to an all-electric heat pump?** For San Jose homeowners in 2026, the answer is increasingly the heat pump — but not for everyone. Here's the honest breakdown we give our own customers.

## The short answer

San Jose's climate is almost ideal for heat pumps. Winters rarely drop below 35°F, which means a modern inverter heat pump runs at high efficiency all season — heating your home 2–4× more efficiently than a gas furnace burns therms. And unlike a furnace, the same equipment cools your home all summer.

## Real cost comparison

| | Gas furnace (96% AFUE) | Heat pump (whole-home) |
|---|---|---|
| Installed cost | ~$9,000–$13,000 | ~$13,000–$20,000 |
| Rebates & credits | Up to ~$600 | Utility rebates (SVCE/PCE/PG&E) vary; the federal 25C credit ended for installs after 2025 and single-family HEEHRA funding is fully reserved |
| Also replaces your AC? | No | **Yes** |
| Monthly winter bill | Rising with PG&E gas rates | Lower for most homes, especially with solar |

The sticker price gap looks big — until you remember the heat pump **replaces both your furnace and your air conditioner**, and rebates can shave thousands off. If your AC is also 12+ years old, the heat pump usually wins on total cost.

## When a furnace still makes sense

- Your AC is newer and you only need heat.
- Your electrical panel is maxed out and an upgrade isn't in the budget (though 120V-friendly and load-managed options are improving fast).
- You want the absolute lowest upfront cost and plan to sell soon.

## When the heat pump wins

- Both your furnace and AC are aging — one system replaces both.
- You have (or plan to add) solar panels — your heating becomes nearly free.
- You want incentives: what's left in 2026 is utility-level money, and it [only applies to heat pumps](/financing), not gas furnaces. The federal 25C credit ended for installations after December 31, 2025, single-family HEEHRA funding is fully reserved statewide (waitlist only), and TECH Clean California is exhausted — we check every active program at your estimate.
- You care about air quality: no combustion in the home means no carbon monoxide risk.
